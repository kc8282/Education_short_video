# Isometric picture-book pitfalls

## Learned teaching revisions (not a story template)

1. **Main teaching group too small:** a beautiful remote place with tiny rows is not a primer. Aim for object grouping 35–45% frame height and confirm individual tokens/shapes at full resolution. Cut nonessential scenery rather than scaling the lesson into decorative space.
2. **Text overlapping geometric subject:** "circle / shape / triangle", digits and arrows can land directly on illustrative bricks and hide them. All labels sit outside the shape **and its outline** in a reserved ground band or safe top/bottom region. Text never overlaps text either; subtitles/KLP notice also get reserved boxes. Approx clear intention is insufficient: measure and sample, preferably every output frame.
3. **Whole-rule half-taught:** if both shape and colour vary, narrating only shape does not define all "patterns". Either hold other attributes constant or describe the dual rule. Name one item's ordinal and the expected item if using a counterexample, while keeping behavior visibly correct before/after.
4. **Spoilers in earlier segments:** a correct sequence in S1 should remain correct; don't reveal the later S3 anomaly before explaining the repeating unit. Later segment's descriptive title overlay is also a spoiler.
5. **Premature verdict:** a rendered partial timestamp or missing marker is not a passed mockup. Rename/re-render, match geometry checks to the right revision, keep `COO-GATE.md` empty.

## Projection and occlusion

- Sort by the object's floor footprint, not a tall centre with z included as if it were x+y. People at their feet can sort differently from a box; apply bias deliberately.
- Tall walls, vehicles or counters remove the field of view behind them along x/y direction. Labels beside a seemingly clear screen point can be obscured by height. Cut away or move labels to the camera side, not simply increase z-order.
- Back-view people cannot walk toward the camera without violating the grammar. Routes follow world axes, away/up-left/up-right or across, with no zigzag noise.
- Bare colour shading may go violet/gray. Use a warm-tone shading function; avoid highlight glints. Flat paper is not generic rendered plastic.
- Boxes can look like repeated floor words; keep background decoration minimal and out of every semantic label. Unintended seams between separately exported frames are common; use one final render.

## Time and evidence

- Always review beginning/middle/end of key label/dissolve/change/wipe/actor movement, not only a pleasant first frame.
- A whole-film camera transform applies to annotation, previous subtitle, subject, arrow. No separately positioned little label surviving after camera moves.
- There should be no black/blank/NaN frame between segment transitions. Ensure label/trial-notice fully arrives before expected read-hold and final voice completes before video cuts off.
- Preserve current audio outputs from final script and no extra duplicate VO duplicated in both mix file and source. Decode+loudness+codec are sanity tests, not an educational or audio listening substitute.
- Record exact geometric audit scope: all output frames vs sparse samples. A program cannot assert every glyph is meaningful merely because rectangles do not intersect; COO still visually reviews.
